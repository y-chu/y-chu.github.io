## Teaching

I teach quantitative methods by making the reasoning behind an analysis visible. I build new concepts from what students already understand, connect intuition to substantive examples, formal definitions, and code, and use hands-on practice to reveal where uncertainty remains. Students may need different entry points, but they work toward the same standards: a defensible question, an appropriate method, reproducible analysis, and an interpretation that states both what the evidence supports and what it does not.

## Mentoring

My mentoring moves students from shared expectations to transferable research judgment and responsible ownership. I make goals, deliverables, communication, documentation, and authorship expectations explicit; teach students to question how data were produced and compare analytical choices; and shift responsibility as they become ready to identify problems, justify decisions, and maintain a research record that others can inspect and continue.

- **2015–2018 · Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health:** As a full-time Research Associate, I recruited, interviewed, and supervised more than ten graduate research assistants and managed their day-to-day work across literature review, data extraction, analysis, modeling support, and visualization. I also mentored two undergraduate students through the Diversity Summer Internship Program.
- **2019–2025 · The Ohio State University and Africa Health Research Institute:** I provided day-to-day mentoring to undergraduate and doctoral research assistants participating in faculty-led research projects, including work on data-quality assessment, data cleaning, coding, and reproducible research workflows.
